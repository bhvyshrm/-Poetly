Salon Folio #09

The Architecture of Nightfall

"for those who listen to the rain at 3 a.m., when the city turns to glass"

We dismantled the clocktower before dawn,
fearing the copper gears would tell the frost
how long we lingered by the canal gates.
Nothing remains unbroken here save the tide.

You said the sky had forgotten its consonants—
only vowels of wet slate and chimney soot
whispering through the dormer windows.
I listened until my fingers turned to salt.

Under the lamps, the wet asphalt glistens
like freshly bound vellum; each footprint an unreadable accent mark
cast into the sanctuary of the night.

Petrichor & Cedar

The first drops hit the needles before they touch earth,
sifting down like green smoke into the damp hollows.
We stood under the canopy without speaking,
counting the heartbeats between thunderclaps.

There is an ancient arithmetic to wet bark,
a sum that only moss and cold air understand.
Take whatever solitude the squall affords you.

Cartography of Stray Hours

A micro-ballad for the quiet hours.

I mapped the room by candle flicker:
two paces to the open window,
four to the stack of unsent letters.
Beyond that, only Atlantic wind and the turning world.
